1 Introduction

Random tensor networks [1] are amazing toy models that describe how effective bulk gravitational degrees of freedom are encoded in the boundary. While very nice and easy to deal with, there are deep conceptual problems when we push these models beyond their limits. This week, I discuss some of these problems and review the various proposed solutions. There are three somewhat related problems that conventional random tensor networks suffer from: (a) flat entanglement spectrum, (b) trivial area operators, and (c) no-go bulk time evolution. These problems ultimately stem from failing to incorporate bulk gauge constraints (diff invariance). On top of that, the naive way of incorporating bulk gauge constraints is like shooting ourselves in the foot because of a no-go theorem proved in [2].

The flat entanglement spectrum problem is fairly simple to understand. Following Calabrese and Cardy [3], the Rényi entropy of the ground state of a (1 + 1)-dimensional CFT is

Sn(A) = c 6 (1 + 1 n ) log (l 𝜖 ) + cn′, (1)

where l is the length of A (which itself is a single spacelike interval), 𝜖 is a UV cutoff, and cn′ are theory-dependent factors. Conventional random tensor networks, on the other hand, give rise to

Sn(A) = |γ| log(d) + O(1); (2)

Rényi entropies that do not depend on n. Such a spectrum of Rényi entropies is called a flat entanglement spectrum, and this conflict with the CFT result needs to be resolved if we are to take random tensor networks seriously.
